# Pull Quote Selections for Enhancement 3

## Implementation Notes for Claude Code

Each unit has **one pull quote per domain section** (Philosophy, Visual Art, Music), selected from the existing content. Pull quotes should be styled as described in the Accessibility and Design Action Plan (Enhancement 3): slightly larger font, left border in the unit's accent color, generous margin, source attribution beneath.

**Only the Philosophy sections contain quotable primary-source passages.** The Visual Art and Music sections are analytical rather than quotation-driven, so pull quotes for those domains are drawn from the site's own analytical prose — the strongest single-sentence insights that anchor each section's argument.

Format for each entry below:
- **Quote**: The exact text to pull
- **Attribution**: How to credit it
- **Location**: Where in the page content the quote currently appears (for reference)
- **Section**: Which domain section it belongs to

## Unit 00: The High Middle Ages

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "God is always quite unable to be comprehended by the first faculty, that of intelligence, but he is totally and perfectly comprehensible by the second, the power of love."

**Attribution**: *The Cloud of Unknowing*, c. 1375

**Location**: Knowledge and Its Limits section

**Rationale**: Captures the medieval epistemological hierarchy — love over intellect — in a single, elegant sentence. Sets the baseline that every subsequent period will challenge.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "These painters were not failing to achieve realism; they were not interested in realism. Their goal was to make the invisible visible — to depict spiritual truths that transcend the physical world."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose)

**Location**: Introduction section

**Rationale**: Immediately corrects the most common student misconception about medieval art.

### Music Pull Quote

**Quote**: "There is no harmony in the modern sense, no bass line, no chords, no accompaniment. Just a single human voice moving through a sacred text."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose)

**Location**: Introduction section

**Rationale**: Makes the strangeness of plainchant vivid and immediate.

## Unit 01: Renaissance & Reformation

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "We have given you, O Adam, no visage proper to yourself, nor endowment properly your own, in order that whatever place, whatever form, whatever gifts you may, with premeditation, select, these same you may have and possess through your own judgment and decision."

**Attribution**: Giovanni Pico della Mirandola, "Oration on the Dignity of Man," 1486

**Location**: The Human Position section

**Rationale**: The single most important passage in the entire course — the moment the medieval framework cracks open. Every student should encounter this as a set piece.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "To paint a measurable space with individual human beings standing in it was to declare that the physical world, and the individual's experience of it, mattered."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose)

**Location**: Introduction section

**Rationale**: Connects the technical achievement of perspective to the philosophical revolution.

### Music Pull Quote

**Location**: Texture & Voices section

## Unit 02: The Baroque

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "Man is but a reed, the most feeble thing in nature; but he is a thinking reed. The entire universe need not arm itself to crush him. A vapour, a drop of water suffices to kill him. But, if the universe were to crush him, man would still be more noble than that which killed him, because he knows that he dies and the advantage which the universe has over him; the universe knows nothing of this."

**Attribution**: Blaise Pascal, *Pensées*, 1670

**Location**: The Human Position section (the passage appears in the epigraph and is discussed in the body)

**Rationale**: The defining passage of the Baroque — grandeur and wretchedness as two faces of the same condition. This is long for a pull quote but it's a single, indivisible thought and one of the most famous passages in European philosophy.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "A single beam of light picks out the crucial action from enveloping darkness."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, describing Baroque chiaroscuro)

**Location**: Light & Shadow section / thread summary

**Rationale**: The most concise possible description of Baroque light. Pairs perfectly with the Caravaggio hero image.

### Music Pull Quote

**Quote**: "The intellectual rigor of the fugue filled with exuberant decorative energy."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, describing Baroque musical structure)

**Location**: Structure & Freedom section

**Rationale**: Captures the Baroque paradox — strict form and ornamental excess — in one phrase.

## Unit 03: The Enlightenment

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "Enlightenment is man's emergence from his self-imposed immaturity. Immaturity is the inability to use one's understanding without guidance from another."

**Attribution**: Immanuel Kant, "What is Enlightenment?", 1784

**Location**: The Human Position section (opening)

**Rationale**: The period's manifesto. Kant's definition is the single most-quoted sentence in Enlightenment philosophy and sets the terms for everything that follows.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "Figures inhabit intimate, ornate interiors and idealized gardens — social spaces where people perform for each other, defined by relationships rather than cosmic forces."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose)

**Location**: Figure & Space thread summary

**Rationale**: Connects Rococo visual space directly to the social world of *Pride and Prejudice*.

### Music Pull Quote

**Quote**: "A single clear melody with supporting accompaniment, like a protagonist's consciousness with other characters arranged around her."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, describing Classical homophony)

**Location**: Texture & Voices section

**Rationale**: The Austen analogy makes the musical concept immediately accessible.

## Unit 04: Romanticism

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "Reason is the enumeration of qualities already known; imagination is the perception of the value of those qualities, both separately and as a whole. Reason respects the differences, and imagination the similitudes of things."

**Attribution**: Percy Bysshe Shelley, *A Defence of Poetry*, 1821

**Location**: Knowledge and Its Limits section

**Rationale**: The clearest statement of the Romantic epistemological revolution — reason analyzes, imagination synthesizes. Pairs with the course's emphasis on *Moby-Dick* as a novel that tries to hold both together.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "The paint does not merely depict a storm; it *behaves* like one."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, describing Romantic brushwork)

**Location**: Brushwork & Surface section

**Rationale**: The single most vivid sentence in the art sections. Makes the Romantic principle of embodiment immediately felt.

### Music Pull Quote

**Quote**: "An unbearable tension that the inherited systems of resolution cannot contain."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, on Wagner and Romantic dissonance)

**Location**: Consonance & Dissonance section

**Rationale**: Works as a description of both Wagnerian harmony and Ahab's obsession — the dual register is the point.

## Unit 05: Modernism

### Philosophy Pull Quote

**Quote**: "It is precisely this sense of being seen without being recognized — of existing as a visible surface onto which others project their assumptions, fears, and fantasies — that Ellison's narrator confronts from the novel's opening line."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, connecting Du Bois to Ellison)

**Note**: If the site's text doesn't contain this exact sentence (I'm reconstructing from the content's argument), use the following from Du Bois instead:

**Alternate quote**: "One ever feels his two-ness — an American, a Negro; two souls, two thoughts, two unreconciled strivings; two warring ideals in one dark body, whose dogged strength alone keeps it from being torn asunder."

**Alternate attribution**: W.E.B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk*, 1903

**Location**: The Human Position section

**Rationale**: Du Bois's "double consciousness" is the intellectual key to *Invisible Man* and the most powerful passage in the Modernism readings. If using the site's own prose, pick the sentence that most directly connects Du Bois's concept to Ellison's narrator.

### Visual Art Pull Quote

**Quote**: "A painting is a flat surface covered with paint, and the artist's gesture is now everything."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, on Modernist surface)

**Location**: Brushwork & Surface thread summary

**Rationale**: The most concise statement of the Modernist revolution in art — the surface becomes the subject.

### Music Pull Quote

**Quote**: "There is no home key, no hierarchy, no resolution. Music sounds permanently unanchored."

**Attribution**: (no attribution — site's own prose, on Schoenberg and atonality)

**Location**: Consonance & Dissonance section

**Rationale**: Captures the Modernist musical crisis in terms that echo the philosophical crisis (no foundations, no certainty).
