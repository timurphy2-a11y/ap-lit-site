# Editorial Rules — AP Lit Site Content Pass

Approved rules for the Phase 4a editorial pass on all 18 existing domain pages (6 units × 3 domains). Apply these before drafting any new content.

---

## The 8 Rules

**Rule 1: Philosophy introductions — cap at 200 words.**
The hub page handles scene-setting. The domain introduction should focus on connecting the readings to the three threads, not re-establishing the period's atmosphere. Primary targets: Enlightenment (346 words → 200) and Modernism (442 words → 200).

**Rule 2: "Connection to [anchor text]" subsections — cap at 120 words (philosophy).**
State the link and why it matters in 2–3 sentences. No mini-essays. Primary targets: Enlightenment philosophy Thread 1 (253 words), Modernism philosophy Thread 3 (222 words), Romanticism philosophy Thread 3 (162 words).

*Retired for art and music (July 30, 2026):* the 100-word cap on art/music "Connection to" sections no longer applies. It was set before Painting/Sculpture/Music moved behind clickable "Read the analysis" panels, a format change that substantially reduced the text density a reader actually encounters on first pass. Live Connection sections on both Modernism pages already ran up to double the old cap, predating this ruling, and Tim's decision is that the cap is obsolete rather than that those pages are non-compliant. Do not enforce this cap on art/music pages, and do not open a remediation pass across the other five units. Philosophy keeps its two-column layout and its 120-word cap unchanged.

**Rule 3: "Looking Back, Looking Forward" sections — cap at 200 words (philosophy) / 100 words (art and music).**
Make the point and stop. Check each "Looking Back" paragraph against the introduction — if it covers the same ground, cut it.

**Rule 4: Don't repeat the hub page.**
Domain introductions should not re-explain the period's general character (wars, crises, atmosphere) — the hub handles that. Open instead by connecting the domain directly to the period and anchor text: "Baroque painting dramatizes..." not "The Thirty Years' War created..."

**Rule 5: Don't repeat between thread sections.**
If the introduction already explains a concept, thread sections should reference it briefly rather than re-explain it. Use bridging phrases: "the sublime — described above — takes a specific form here..."

**Rule 6: Preserve "What to Notice" / "What to Listen For" sections at full length.**
These are the most pedagogically immediate parts of the site. Do not cut.

**Rule 7: Preserve thread section core arguments at full length.**
The analytical paragraphs (not "Connection to" or "Looking Back/Forward") are the intellectual backbone. Tighten prose quality, but don't reduce substance. Cuts come from the surrounding apparatus, not the core argument.

**Rule 8: Modernism painting and music pages are exempt from length reduction.**
At 1,514 and 1,402 words respectively, they're within the target range already and cover more ground (multiple movements, European/African American duality).
